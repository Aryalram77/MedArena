🧠Brainstem Syndromes & Mnemonics

1Fundamental Rules of Brainstem Localization

A. The "Rule of 4" (Cranial Nerve Levels)

The 12 cranial nerves are divided across the brainstem:

  • Midbrain (Upper): Cranial Nerves 3 and 4
    (CN 1 and 2 originate directly from the forebrain/diencephalon).
  • Pons (Middle): Cranial Nerves 5, 6, 7, and 8
  • Medulla (Lower): Cranial Nerves 9, 10, 11, and 12

B. The "Rule of 12" (Medial Cranial Nerves)

  • Cranial nerves whose numbers divide evenly into 12 are located medially:

3,4,6,12

  • 3×4=12
  • 4×3=12
  • 6×2=12
  • 12×1=12
  • All other cranial nerves (5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11) exit laterally.

C. The "Rule of M" (Medial Brainstem Structures)

Everything located in the medial aspect starts with M:

  • Motor Cranial Nerves:
    • CN 3, 4, 6: Eye movement
    • CN 12: Tongue movement
  • Motor Pathway:
    • Corticospinal Tract (CST): Lesion causes contralateral hemiplegia / hemiparesis.
  • Medial Lemniscus (Dorsal Column Pathway):
    • Carries conscious proprioception, vibration, and fine touch
    • → Lesion causes contralateral loss of proprioception/vibration.
  • Medial Longitudinal Fasciculus (MLF):
    • Coordinates horizontal conjugate eye movements.

D. The "Rule of S" (Side / Lateral Brainstem Structures)

Everything located on the side (lateral) aspect starts with S:

  • Sensory Cranial Nerves:
    • CN 5 (trigeminal – facial sensation), CN 7, 8 (auditory/vestibular), CN 9, 10, 11.
  • Spinothalamic Tract (SPT):
    • Carries pain and temperature
    • → Crosses early in spinal cord
    • → Lesion causes contralateral loss of pain and temperature over the body.
  • Sympathetic Pathway:
    • Disruption causes ipsilateral Horner syndrome (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis).
  • Spinocerebellar / Cerebellar Pathways:
    • Disruption causes ipsilateral cerebellar ataxia.

E. General Deficit Lateralization

  • Cranial Nerve deficits are always IPSILATERAL to the lesion.
  • Long tract motor/sensory deficits (CST / Spinothalamic) are CONTRALATERAL.

Crossed Sensory Deficit (Alternating Hemianesthesia)

  • Seen in lateral brainstem lesions.
  • Ipsilateral face sensory loss (CN 5 spinal tract/nucleus).
  • Contralateral body pain & temperature loss (Spinothalamic tract).

2Midbrain Syndromes

All midbrain vascular syndromes involve an ipsilateral 3rd cranial nerve (Oculomotor) palsy and are typically supplied by penetrating branches of the Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA).

Midbrain Syndromes = Ipsilateral CN 3 Palsy + Characteristic Feature

1. Weber Syndrome

  • Localization: Ventral midbrain / Cerebral peduncle.
  • Mnemonic: Weber = Weakness.

Features

  • Ipsilateral CN 3 palsy.
  • Contralateral hemiparesis / hemiplegia (involvement of Corticospinal Tract in cerebral peduncle).

2. Benedikt Syndrome

  • Localization: Midbrain tegmentum involving the Red Nucleus.
  • Mnemonic: Bened = Red nucleus; Benedikt = Bobbing head / involuntary movements.

Features

  • Ipsilateral CN 3 palsy.
  • Contralateral tremor, involuntary choreiform/athetoid movements, or ataxia (dentatorubrothalamic tract / red nucleus).

3. Nothnagel Syndrome

  • Localization: Superior Cerebellar Peduncle (SCP).
  • Mnemonic: North = Superior cerebellar peduncle.

Features

  • Ipsilateral CN 3 palsy.
  • Cerebellar ataxia (loss of coordination).

4. Claude Syndrome

  • Formula:

B+N=C

(Benedikt + Nothnagel = Claude).

  • Localization: Involves both the Red Nucleus and Superior Cerebellar Peduncle.

Features

  • Ipsilateral CN 3 palsy.
  • Contralateral extrapyramidal signs/tremor (Benedikt component).
  • Cerebellar ataxia (Nothnagel component).

5. Parinaud Syndrome (Dorsal Midbrain / Tectal Syndrome)

  • Etiology: Pineal gland tumor / germinoma compressing the dorsal midbrain (tectum/colliculi).
  • Mnemonic: Parinaud = Pineal tumor / Dorsal midbrain.
  • Involvement: Superior colliculi and pretectal area.

Features

  • Upward gaze palsy (vertical gaze center affected; "sunset sign").
  • Convergence-retraction nystagmus.
  • Lid retraction (Collier sign).
  • Light-near dissociation (pseudo-Argyll Robertson pupil: reacts to accommodation but not to light).

3Pontine Syndromes

Pontine cranial nerves are 5, 6, 7, and 8 (CN 6 is medial; CN 5, 7, 8 are lateral).

1. Marie-Foix Syndrome (Lateral Pontine Syndrome)

  • Vascular Supply: AICA (Anterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery, branch of the Basilar artery).
  • Mnemonic: Foix = Facial (CN 7) + hearing loss (CN 8).
    (Also: "AICA" sounds like the Marathi word for "listen", linking AICA to CN 8 / hearing).

Features

  • Ipsilateral facial weakness/paralysis (CN 7).
  • Ipsilateral hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo, nystagmus (CN 8 / labyrinthine artery).
  • Ipsilateral loss of facial sensation (CN 5).
  • Contralateral hemiplegia (CST).
  • Contralateral loss of pain and temperature on body (SPT).
  • Ipsilateral limb ataxia.
  • Ipsilateral Horner syndrome.

2. Millard-Gubler Syndrome vs. Foville Syndrome

Both involve CN 6 + CN 7 + CST (contralateral hemiplegia), but they differ by the exact lesion site of CN 6:

3. Raymond Syndrome

  • Mnemonic: Ray = X-ray
  • → X = Roman numeral VI (6 only).

Features

  • Ipsilateral CN 6 palsy (lateral rectus weakness).
  • Contralateral hemiparesis (CST).
  • Spares CN 7.

4. Locked-in Syndrome

  • Etiology: Acute bilateral ventral pontine infarction due to Basilar artery thrombosis.
  • Mnemonic: Locked in the Basement
  • → Basilar artery occlusion.

Features

  • Complete quadriplegia and anarthria/aphonia (loss of all voluntary motor function).
  • Preserved consciousness (Reticular Activating System in tegmentum is intact).
  • Preserved vertical eye movements and blinking (supranuclear centers in midbrain spared).

4Medullary Syndromes

Medullary cranial nerves are 9, 10, 11, and 12 (CN 12 is medial; CN 9, 10, 11 are lateral).

Medulla:
- Medial: CN 12 + CST + Medial Lemniscus
- Lateral: CN 9, 10, 11 + CN 5 + Spinothalamic + Sympathetic + Cerebellar

1. Medial Medullary Syndrome (Dejerine Syndrome)

  • Vascular Supply: Anterior Spinal Artery (ASA) or branch of Vertebral Artery.
  • Mnemonic: MD = Medial is Dejerine.

Features

  • Ipsilateral CN 12 palsy: Weakness and atrophy of the tongue.
    • Mnemonic: "Tongue licks the wound"
    • → Tongue deviates toward the side of the lesion.
  • Contralateral hemiplegia: Corticospinal tract involvement.
  • Contralateral loss of proprioception/vibration: Medial lemniscus involvement.

(Note: Do not confuse Dejerine Medullary Syndrome with Dejerine-Roussy Syndrome, which is a thalamic pain syndrome).

2. Lateral Medullary Syndrome (Wallenberg Syndrome)

  • Vascular Supply: Vertebral Artery (→ Posterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery / PICA).
  • Mnemonic: PICA-chew
  • → Chewing/swallowing/speech difficulty (CN 9 & 10).

Structures Involved

  • Nucleus Ambiguus (CN 9, 10, 11): Dysphagia, dysarthria, hoarseness, loss of gag reflex, uvula deviates to the opposite side.
  • Spinal Trigeminal Tract/Nucleus (CN 5): Ipsilateral facial loss of pain & temperature.
  • Spinothalamic Tract: Contralateral body loss of pain & temperature.
  • Vestibular Nuclei: Vertigo, nausea, vomiting, nystagmus.
  • Inferior Cerebellar Peduncle: Ipsilateral limb ataxia.
  • Descending Sympathetics: Ipsilateral Horner syndrome.

Critical Negative Findings

  • NO hemiplegia (CST is medial).
  • NO tongue deviation (CN 12 is medial).

5Clinical Integration: Taste Pathway

Clinical Question:
Which brainstem syndrome causes loss of taste from the anterior 2/3 of the tongue?

Innervation Pathway

  • Taste from anterior 2/3 of tongue
  • → CN 7 (Chorda tympani).
  • Taste from posterior 1/3 of tongue
  • → CN 9.
  • Taste from epiglottis/vallecula
  • → CN 10.
  • All taste fibers terminate in the Nucleus Tractus Solitarius (NTS) (N = 9, T = 10, S = 7).

Answer

  • Because the anterior 2/3 taste is mediated by CN 7, it is impaired in Lateral Pontine Syndrome (Marie-Foix syndrome / AICA occlusion).

6Internuclear Ophthalmoplegia (INO) & the MLF

Anatomy of Horizontal Gaze

  • To look to one side (e.g., to the left):
    • Left CN 6 nucleus activates the Left Lateral Rectus (abduction).
    • Left CN 6 nucleus simultaneously sends an interneuron through the Right MLF across the midline to the Right CN 3 subnucleus (Medial Rectus).
    • Right Medial Rectus adducts the right eye.
Right Eye (Adduction)  <--- [Right MLF] <--- [Left CN 6 Nucleus] ---> Left Eye (Abduction)
(CN 3)                                                              (CN 6)

Pathophysiology of INO

  • The MLF is named by the adduction deficit:

Right MLF Lesion

  • When asked to look to the Left:
    • Right eye cannot adduct (failure of right medial rectus).
    • Left eye abducts with nystagmus (compensatory overactivation).

Primary Gaze

  • Mnemonic: I-NO = In primary gaze, NO abnormality (eyes look straight ahead with no squint).

Etiology

  • Young patients / Bilateral INO: Multiple Sclerosis (demyelinating plaque in MLF).
  • Elderly patients / Unilateral INO: Brainstem Stroke (ischemia to paramedian pontine perforators of basilar artery).

1Primary Auditory Cortex & Heschl’s Gyrus

📝 Exam Check

Heschl’s gyrus is located in:

  1. A. Primary auditory cortex
  2. B. Visual cortex
  3. C. Parietal association area
  4. D. Medial frontal lobe
✔ Answer: A. Primary auditory cortex

Key Anatomical Facts & Mnemonics

  • Mnemonic: HEschl’s
  • → HEaring.
  • Location: Superior surface of the Superior Temporal Gyrus (within the lateral sulcus/transverse temporal gyrus).
  • Brodmann Areas: Area 41 and 42 (Primary Auditory Cortex).

3Frontal Eye Field & Summary of Key Brodmann Areas

📝 Exam Check

Frontal eye field is Brodmann area:

  1. A. 4
  2. B. 8
  3. C. 41, 42
  4. D. 17
✔ Answer: B. 8

High-Yield Brodmann Areas & Mnemonics

Frontal Cortex Motor Areas (Even Numbers):
Area 4 (Primary Motor) ---> Area 6 (Premotor) ---> Area 8 (Frontal Eye Field)

Frontal Eye Field (Area 8)

  • Mnemonic: Eye field looks like a pair of glasses/spectacles
  • → shaped like the number 8.
  • Cross-topic pearl: Mahatma Gandhi (MG = Myasthenia Gravis) wore round spectacles
  • → HLA-B8 association.

Primary Sensory Cortex (Areas 3, 1, 2)

  • Postcentral gyrus (Parietal lobe).
  • Sensation comes first before movement
  • → Count 1, 2, 3.

Primary Motor Cortex (Area 4)

  • Precentral gyrus (Frontal lobe).
  • Motor control of our 4 limbs
  • → Area 4.

Premotor / Supplementary Motor Cortex (Area 6)

  • Located anterior to Area 4.

Primary Visual Cortex (Area 17)

  • Located in the occipital lobe (calcarine sulcus).
  • Mnemonic: Seeing is Visual
  • → Seeing = Seventeen (Area 17).

4Quadrigeminal Cistern & Vein of Galen

📝 Exam Check

Quadrigeminal cistern contains:

  1. A. Occipital artery
  2. B. Middle cerebral artery
  3. C. Vein of Galen
  4. D. Superior sagittal sinus
✔ Answer: C. Vein of Galen

Anatomy of the Cistern

  • Boundaries: Located dorsal to the midbrain colliculi (quadrigeminal plate / tectum) and inferior to the splenium of the corpus callosum.
  • Content: Great Cerebral Vein of Galen.
  • Venous Drainage Pathway:
Two Internal Cerebral Veins (ICVs)+Basal Veins (of Rosenthal) → Vein of Galen → Straight SinusTwo Internal Cerebral Veins (ICVs)+Basal Veins (of Rosenthal) → Vein of Galen → Straight Sinus

High-Yield Clinical Pearl: Vein of Galen Malformation (VGAM)

  • An intracranial arteriovenous fistula (AVF) causing dilatation of the median prosencephalic vein.

Classic Presentation in a Newborn

  • High-output congestive heart failure (due to severe arteriovenous shunting).
  • Non-communicating hydrocephalus (aqueductal compression).
  • Cranial bruit.

5Anatomical Relations of the Oculomotor Nerve (CN III)

📝 Exam Check

Identify the nerve emerging between the Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA) and Superior Cerebellar Artery (SCA):

  1. A. Optic nerve
  2. B. Oculomotor nerve
  3. C. Trigeminal nerve
  4. D. Trochlear nerve
✔ Answer: B. Oculomotor nerve

Key Anatomical Relations on the Ventral Brainstem

  • CN III (Oculomotor): Emerges from the interpeduncular fossa of the midbrain and courses directly between the Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA) above and the Superior Cerebellar Artery (SCA) below.

Clinical Significance

  • An aneurysm of the Posterior Communicating Artery (PCoA) or PCA/SCA compresses the outer parasympathetic fibers of CN III first
  • → pupillomotor dysfunction (blown/dilated pupil), followed by complete "down and out" ophthalmoplegia.

Other Nerves on Ventral Brainstem Inspection

  • CN II (Optic): Forms the optic chiasm anteriorly.
  • CN V (Trigeminal): The thickest cranial nerve, exiting laterally from the mid-pons.
  • CN IV (Trochlear): The only cranial nerve emerging from the dorsal aspect of the brainstem.

6Basilar Artery Branches vs. Vertebral Artery Branches

📝 Exam Check

Identify the labeled vessel arising from the inferior third of the Basilar Artery:

  1. A. Basilar artery
  2. B. PICA
  3. C. AICA
  4. D. SCA
✔ Answer: C. AICA

Mnemonic for Basilar Artery Branches: "PAS-ilar"

From superior to inferior:

  • PCA (Posterior Cerebral Artery) – terminal bifurcation
  • SCA (Superior Cerebellar Artery) – arises just before termination
  • AICA (Anterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery) – arises from the inferior basilar trunk

Critical Distinction

  • AICA arises from the Basilar Artery.
  • PICA (Posterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery) arises from the Vertebral Artery.
  • Occlusion of PICA
  • → Lateral Medullary (Wallenberg) Syndrome.

7Ventricular System & Axial Midbrain Anatomy

📝 Exam Check

Cavity of the mesencephalon is the:

  1. A. Lateral ventricle
  2. B. Third ventricle
  3. C. Aqueduct of Sylvius
  4. D. Fourth ventricle
✔ Answer: C. Aqueduct of Sylvius

Brain Vesicles and Their Cavities

  • Telencephalon: Lateral ventricles
  • Diencephalon: Third ventricle
  • Mesencephalon (Midbrain): Cerebral Aqueduct of Sylvius (connects 3rd and 4th ventricles)
  • Rhombencephalon (Pons/Medulla/Cerebellum): Fourth ventricle

Axial Midbrain on MRI ("Mickey Mouse" Appearance)

  • Cerebral Peduncles: Mickey's "ears".
  • Aqueduct of Sylvius: Small rounded opening in the midline posterior tegmentum.

Mammillary Bodies

  • Located immediately anterior to the midbrain.
  • Mnemonic: MB is in front of MB
  • → Mammillary Bodies are in front of the MidBrain.
  • Pineal Gland: Located posterior to the midbrain / above the tectal plate.

8Hypothalamic Nuclei Functions

📝 Exam Check

All of the following pairs are correct for nuclei of the hypothalamus EXCEPT:

  1. A. Ventromedial: Hunger
  2. B. Supra-optic: Water conservation
  3. C. Posterior nucleus: Shivering center
  4. D. Suprachiasmatic: Circadian rhythm
✔ Answer: A. Ventromedial: Hunger (Incorrect; Ventromedial = Satiety)

High-Yield Mnemonics for Hypothalamic Nuclei

- Lateral Nucleus: Hunger (Eat wide/lateral)
- Ventromedial Nucleus: Satiety (Full inside/medial)

Feeding Centers

  • Lateral Nucleus: Controls Hunger / appetite stimulation.
    (Destruction
  • → Anorexia, starvation).
  • Ventromedial Nucleus: Controls Satiety.
    (Destruction
  • → Hyperphagia, obesity).

Posterior Pituitary Hormone Secretion ("V for O and O for V"):

  • Ventricular (Paraventricular)
  • → Secretes Oxytocin.
  • Optic (Supraoptic)
  • → Secretes Vasopressin (ADH
  • → water conservation).

Temperature Regulation

  • Anterior Nucleus: Detects Heat
  • → Dissipates heat (cooling center / Parasympathetic).
    Mnemonic: HeANT
  • → Heat is controlled by Anterior (A/C).
  • Posterior Nucleus: Detects Cold
  • → Conserves heat (shivering center / Sympathetic).

Circadian Rhythm

  • Suprachiasmatic Nucleus (SCN):
    Mnemonic: Chiasmatic
  • → Circadian rhythm (driven by light inputs from the retina).

9The Auditory Pathway

📝 Exam Check

Which of the following is NOT a component of the auditory pathway?

  1. A. Inferior colliculus
  2. B. Lateral geniculate body
  3. C. Superior olivary nucleus
  4. D. Lateral lemniscus
✔ Answer: B. Lateral geniculate body (LGB is Visual)

Mnemonics for the Auditory Pathway: "E. COLI MA" / "SLIM"

Cochlear Nerve ---> Superior Olivary Complex ---> Lateral Lemniscus ---> Inferior Colliculus ---> Medial Geniculate Body ---> Auditory Cortex
  • Superior olivary complex
  • Lateral lemniscus
  • Inferior colliculus
  • Medial geniculate body (MGB)

Geniculate Body Mnemonic

  • MGB (Medial Geniculate Body): Medial = Music / Gaana
  • → Hearing (Auditory).
  • LGB (Lateral Geniculate Body): Lateral = Light
  • → Vision (Visual).

10Brown-Séquard Syndrome (Spinal Cord Hemisection)

📝 Exam Check

In Brown-Séquard syndrome, which of the following is FALSE?

  1. A. Ipsilateral loss of vibration
  2. B. Ipsilateral loss of pain & temperature
  3. C. Ipsilateral loss of motor function
  4. D. Ipsilateral loss of fine touch
✔ Answer: B. Ipsilateral loss of pain & temperature (It is Contralateral)

High-Yield Rule: "OPT"

  • Mnemonic: OPT
  • → OPposite side loses Pain and Temperature.
  • Mechanism: The lateral spinothalamic tract decussates 1–2 segments above entry in the spinal cord; therefore, a unilateral lesion damages fibers that ascended from the contralateral side.

Deficit Patterns in Cord Hemisection

Modality / PathwaySide of Deficit Relative to LesionLevel of Deficit
Pain & Temperature (Spinothalamic)Contralateral (Opposite)1–2 segments below the lesion level
Motor / Voluntary Movement (Corticospinal)Ipsilateral (Same side)Below lesion level (UMN signs); at level (LMN signs)
Vibration, Proprioception, Fine Touch (Dorsal Columns)Ipsilateral (Same side)Below lesion level

Quick Summary Cheat Sheet

  • Heschl’s Gyrus: Area 41, 42
  • → Primary Auditory.
  • Wernicke’s: Area 22
  • → Superior Temporal (WEST).
  • Broca’s: Area 44, 45
  • → Inferior Frontal (BIF).
  • Frontal Eye Field: Area 8 (Spectacles shape).
  • Primary Visual: Area 17 (Seeing = Seventeen).
  • Quadrigeminal Cistern: Houses Vein of Galen (drains to Straight Sinus).
  • CN III Location: Sandwiched between PCA and SCA.
  • Basilar Branches: PAS (PCA, AICA, SCA); PICA is from Vertebral.
  • Aqueduct of Sylvius: Cavity of the midbrain (mesencephalon).
  • Hypothalamus: Lateral = Lunch (Hunger); Medial = Satiety; V for O (Paraventricular = Oxytocin); O for V (Supraoptic = Vasopressin).
  • Auditory Pathway: SLIM; MGB = Music (Auditory); LGB = Light (Vision).
  • Brown-Séquard: OPT (Opposite = Pain & Temperature); all others are Ipsilateral.
