### 1. Atresia of third part of duodenum causes

a. Abdominal pain  
b. Bilious vomiting  
c. Non-bilious vomiting  
d. Diarrhea  

**Answer:** b. Bilious vomiting  

**Explanation:** Duodenal atresia presents with bilious vomiting, because the obstruction is distal to the ampulla of Vater.



Treatment of choice: duodenoduodenostomy (diamond-shaped anastomosis).

Plain X-ray: double-bubble sign with absence of gas in the distal bowel.

Other causes of duodenal obstruction: annular pancreas (treated by duodenoduodenostomy) and Ladd's bands.

### 2. Triad seen in Wilms' tumor includes all except:

a. Hematuria  
b. Fever  
c. Abdominal mass  
d. Oliguria  

**Answer:** d. Oliguria  

**Explanation:** The classic triad of Wilms' tumor is abdominal mass + fever + hematuria.



Wilms' tumor is the most common neoplasm of childhood, occurring at 2-5 years of age.

Hypertension occurs in about 25% of cases due to renin release, but oliguria is not part of the triad.

Associated with deletion of 11p13 (WT1, WT2 genes).

### 3. A child has recurrent UTI without a palpable urinary bladder. The best investigation would be:

a. MCU (micturating cystourethrogram)  
b. CT  
c. USG  
d. MRI  

**Answer:** a. MCU (micturating cystourethrogram)  

**Explanation:** The most common cause of UTI in children is vesicoureteric reflux (VUR).



MCU detects the presence of VUR and other anomalies, especially posterior urethral valves.

Recommended radiological evaluation: USG abdomen (kidney, ureter, bladder), MCU and radionuclide renal scan (99mTc DMSA scan).

A DMSA scan is used to confirm renal scarring and diagnose acute pyelonephritis.

Definitive diagnosis of UTI depends on urine culture showing 10^5 or more bacteria per colony forming unit.

### 4. Commonest fracture in a fall with outstretched hand:

a. Clavicle  
b. Pisiform  
c. Radius  
d. Scaphoid  

**Answer:** a. Clavicle  

**Explanation:** The clavicle is the most commonly fractured bone overall. It usually results from a fall on the shoulder or on an outstretched hand.



The junction of the middle and outer third of the clavicle is the commonest site of fracture.

Fracture of the clavicle unites readily even if displaced.

The clavicle is the only long bone with membranous (endochondral) ossification, so it is the last long bone to fuse.

Note: the scaphoid is the commonest carpal bone fractured in a fall on an outstretched hand.

### 5. A 40-year-old female has a hard breast mass. Which of the following simple investigation can reach the diagnosis?

a. USG  
b. Mammography  
c. FNAC  
d. CT  

**Answer:** b. Mammography  

**Explanation:** Approach to a breast mass:



The first step in the workup of a breast mass is clinical diagnosis, followed by radiological investigation and FNAC (triple assessment for breast cancer).

For a solid mass, mammography is the imaging of choice (two views: craniocaudal and mediolateral oblique).

Mammography is a plain X-ray of soft tissue of the breast using low voltage and high amperage X-rays.

Findings on mammography: microcalcification, mass, architectural distortion, and distortion of calcified areas.

The ultimate diagnostic procedure is biopsy.

### 6. A patient has splenomegaly on palpation, hemoglobin 6 g%, and a high total leukocyte count. Most probable diagnosis?

a. Chronic myeloid leukemia  
b. Hepatic anemia  
c. Aplastic anemia  
d. Multiple myeloma  

**Answer:** a. Chronic myeloid leukemia  

**Explanation:** Marked splenomegaly with anemia and a very high leukocyte count is characteristic of chronic myeloid leukemia (CML).



Blood examination in leukemia shows anemia with normal or raised MCV, a leukocyte count that may vary from as low as 1 x 10^9/L to over 500 x 10^9/L, and thrombocytopenia. Appearance of blast cells in the peripheral blood is diagnostic of acute leukemia.

### 7. Which of the following is a non-nucleated cell?

a. Lymphocyte  
b. RBC  
c. Neutrophil  
d. Monocyte  

**Answer:** b. RBC  

**Explanation:** The red blood cell is anucleate and lacks organelles.



Erythrocytes are biconcave, with a large surface area to volume ratio for rapid gas exchange.

Life span: 120 days.

Source of energy is glucose (90% used in glycolysis, 10% in the HMP shunt).

The red cell membrane contains a Cl-/HCO3- antiporter, which allows the RBC to export HCO3- and transport CO2 from the periphery to the lungs for elimination.

b. ARM (artificial rupture of membranes)

With a dead fetus, the mode of delivery is decided by the grade of placenta previa and the condition of the mother. Prostaglandin induction and ARM are used when there is no contraindication to vaginal delivery.

### 13. The life cycle of which parasite is completed in man?

a. Strongyloides  
b. Hookworm  
c. Ascariasis  
d. Trichuris trichiura  

**Answer:** d. Trichuris trichiura  

**Explanation:** Trichuris trichiura (whipworm) completes its entire life cycle in man, without an intermediate host. The egg develops in soil and is ingested; the larva matures to the adult worm in the caecum and colon.



Strongyloides stercoralis can complete its cycle in man (autoinfection). Hookworm and Ascaris need soil development of the egg before ingestion, but the full parasitic cycle of Ascaris is completed in man and the pig acts as a paratenic host.

### 14. 'Chain of lakes' appearance on ERCP is seen in:

a. Acute pancreatitis  
b. Chronic pancreatitis  
c. Carcinoma pancreas  
d. Ductal adenoma  

**Answer:** b. Chronic pancreatitis  

**Explanation:** Chronic pancreatitis shows a dilated main pancreatic duct with alternating strictures and dilatations, giving a 'chain of lakes' appearance on ERCP.



In acute pancreatitis, the sentinel loop (dilated proximal small bowel) and colon cut-off sign (distention of transverse colon) are seen on X-ray.

Secretin stimulation test is the diagnostic test of chronic pancreatitis with the best sensitivity and specificity.

Serum lipase is the most specific laboratory test for establishing a diagnosis of acute pancreatitis.

a. Fluoxetine  
b. Buspirone  
c. Amitriptyline  
d. Imipramine  

**Answer:** a. Fluoxetine

Note: fluoxetine is a potent CYP2D6 inhibitor, so careful titration and interaction review are needed.

### 21. HIV virus is a:

a. Single-stranded RNA virus  
b. Double-stranded DNA virus  
c. Double-stranded RNA virus  
d. Single-stranded DNA virus  

**Answer:** a. Single-stranded RNA virus  

**Explanation:** HIV is a single-stranded RNA virus (a retrovirus).



It has 3 structural genes: env (gp120 and gp41, formed by cleavage of gp160 to form the envelope glycoprotein, which binds to CD4), gag (p24 and p17 - capsid and matrix proteins) and pol (reverse transcriptase, integrase, protease).

Reverse transcriptase synthesizes dsDNA from the genomic RNA, and the dsDNA integrates into the host genome.

The virus binds CD4 as well as a co-receptor, either CCR5 on macrophages (early infection) or CXCR4 on T-cells (late infection).

### 22. 100% oxygen is not used in:

b. Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF)  
c. Gas gangrene  
d. VSD  

**Answer:** d. VSD

In low concentrations, when there are breathing difficulties due to conditions such as chronic obstructive airways disease (COAD/COPD).

In uncomplicated VSD without hypoxemia or respiratory failure, routine 100% oxygen is not indicated.

### 26. Unidirectional flow of the nerve impulse is at:

a. Synapse  
b. Axon  
c. Dendrites  
d. All of the above  

**Answer:** a. Synapse  

**Explanation:** The nerve impulse travels in one direction only, the most important reason being synaptic transmission. Dendrites pick up the electrical impulse and conduct it to the cell body, then to the axon. At the end of the axon the impulse travels across the gap called the synapse to reach the dendrites of the next cell.



Synapses generally permit conduction of impulses in one direction only, because the neurotransmitter substance is present in vesicles of the presynaptic neuron only.

### 27. Excitable tissue at rest is least permeable to:

a. Na+  
b. K+  
c. Ca2+  
d. Cl-  

**Answer:** a. Na+  

**Explanation:** Under the resting state, the cell membrane is permeable to K+ and hence the resting membrane potential is mainly determined by the permeability to K+.



ICF major cation is K+ and anions are organic anions (protein); ECF major cation is Na+ and major anion is Cl-.

91% of total body sodium is extracellular, of which the maximum is in bone.

Water is freely permeable and urea is the most diffusible substance.

### 31. Full rigor mortis develops in about:

a. 2 hours  
b. 12 hours  
c. 24 hours  
d. 48 hours  

**Answer:** b. 12 hours  

**Explanation:** Rigor mortis usually begins 1-2 hours after death, takes a further 12 hours to develop fully, and lasts 24-48 hours depending on the temperature and the state of the muscles.



Rigor mortis is earlier if the temperature is high and is late in a cold environment.

Involuntary muscles (myocardium of the LV) are involved first, within an hour of death.

### 32. Diatom test is positive in:

a. Wet drowning  
b. Dry drowning  
c. Hanging  
d. Throttling  

**Answer:** a. Wet drowning  

**Explanation:** Finding of diatoms in tissue (especially bone marrow of the femur) is highly suggestive of antemortem drowning.



In typical (wet, primary) drowning, water is inhaled into the lungs.

Diatoms are unicellular or colonial aquatic algae with an extracellular silica coat. The Getter test is also used in drowning.

The difference in chloride content between the right and left chambers of the heart is significant.



Causes of death in drowning: in typical/wet/primary drowning, cardiac arrest due to asystole (in salt water); in dry drowning, reflex laryngospasm; in secondary drowning, hypoxia, ARDS, sepsis and MODS.

### 40. All of the following are true about prevalence except:

a. It is a rate  
b. It includes both new and old cases  
c. Prevalence = Incidence x Duration (in a stable population)  
d. It can be point or period prevalence  

**Answer:** a. It is a rate  

**Explanation:** Prevalence is a proportion (not a true rate) representing all current cases (old and new) existing at a given point in time, or over a period of time, in a given population.



Types of prevalence: point prevalence and period prevalence.

Incidence is a rate: the number of new cases occurring in a defined population during a specified period of time.

Prevalence = Incidence x Duration when the population is stable.

### 41. Berkson's bias refers to:

a. Bias arising from different rates of admission to the hospital  
b. Bias due to recall  
c. Bias due to interviewer  
d. Bias due to loss to follow-up  

**Answer:** a. Bias arising from different rates of admission to the hospital  

**Explanation:** Berkson's bias (admission rate bias) is a selection bias that occurs in hospital-based case-control studies, because the proportion of cases and controls admitted to hospital differs between the exposure and non-exposure groups.



Berkson's bias is also called collider bias.

### 42. Widened anion gap is not seen in:

a. Acute renal failure  
b. Diarrhea  
c. Diabetic ketoacidosis  
d. Lactic acidosis  

**Answer:** b. Diarrhea  

**Explanation:** The normal value for the serum anion gap is 8-16 mEq/L, but a serum anion gap less than 11 mEq/L is considered normal because of unmeasurable ions.



Diarrhea causes a hyperchloremic (normal anion gap) metabolic acidosis due to bicarbonate loss.

Causes of a high anion gap: uremia, diabetic ketoacidosis, lactic acidosis, propylene glycol, isoniazid/iron intoxication, methanol, ethylene glycol and salicylates (MUDPILES).

### 43. Lactic acidosis is not seen in:

a. Methanol poisoning  
b. Respiratory failure  
c. Circulatory failure  
d. Tolbutamide  

**Answer:** b. Respiratory failure  

**Explanation:** Lactic acidosis results from tissue hypoxia and anaerobic glycolysis. It is seen in shock and circulatory failure, and can accompany respiratory failure. It is a characteristic feature of metformin toxicity.



Methanol poisoning causes a high anion gap acidosis from formic acid, and salicylates cause a respiratory alkalosis with a high anion gap.

Note: the answer given in the source paper for this item is (b) respiratory failure; from the standard teaching, lactic acidosis is most characteristic of circulatory failure (shock) and of metformin toxicity.

### 48. Least observed laboratory finding in neonatal sepsis is:

a. C-reactive protein  
b. Neutrophilia  
c. Increased ESR  
d. Increased immature neutrophils  

**Answer:** b. Neutrophilia  

**Explanation:** Neutrophilia is the least characteristic finding in neonatal sepsis. In the neonate the more typical abnormalities are an abnormal immature-to-total neutrophil ratio (left shift), thrombocytopenia, and raised CRP. ESR is not a useful marker in the neonate.



In fact, neutropenia is a common and ominous finding in neonatal sepsis, whereas neutrophilia is more typical of bacterial infection in the older child.

### 49. Defective hepatic conjugation is seen in all of the following except:

a. Neonatal jaundice  
b. Gilbert syndrome  
c. Crigler-Najjar syndrome  
d. Novobiocin therapy

### 97. Case control studies are used for:

a. Study of common diseases  
b. Finding multiple risk factors  
c. Finding incidence rates  
d. Finding relative risk  

**Answer:** b. Finding multiple risk factors  

**Explanation:** A case-control study is commonly the first approach to test a causal hypothesis. Its three distinct features are:



1. Both the exposure (risk factor) and the outcome (disease) have occurred before the study, so there is no further risk for the subjects.

2. The study proceeds backwards from effect to cause, so it is a retrospective study.

3. It uses a control or comparison group to support or refute an inference of difference.



The focus is on disease that has already developed, allows the study of several different etiological factors (eg. smoking, physical activity and personality characteristic in myocardial infarction), and the association between the risk factor and the disease can be tested. A case-control study cannot directly measure incidence or prevalence.

### 99. Sensitivity refers to:

### 100. Carpal tunnel syndrome is due to compression of:

a. Radial nerve  
b. Ulnar nerve  
c. Median nerve  
d. Palmar branch of the ulnar nerve  

**Answer:** c. Median nerve  

**Explanation:** Carpal tunnel syndrome is an entrapment neuropathy of the median nerve in the carpal tunnel.



Common causes: idiopathic (the commonest cause), excessive use of the wrist (as in typists), acromegaly, Cushing's syndrome and pregnancy.



Clinical features: wasting of the thenar muscles (ape-like hand), loss of abduction and opposition of the thumb, nocturnal paresthesia of the thumb, index and middle fingers, and trophic changes (dry scaly skin) in long-standing cases. Phalen's test is used for diagnosis.
