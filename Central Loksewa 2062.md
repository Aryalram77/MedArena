### 1. Atresia of third part of duodenum causes
a. Abdominal pain  
b. Vomiting  
c. Non-bilious vomiting  
d. Diarrhea  

**Answer:** b. Vomiting (bilious vomiting)

**Explanation:** Obstruction at/around the 2nd part of duodenum (ampulla region) classically causes bilious vomiting. Duodenal atresia commonly presents with bilious vomiting. Plain X-ray may show the double-bubble sign with absence/distal paucity of gas. Treatment is typically duodenoduodenostomy. Annular pancreas can also cause duodenal obstruction.

### 2. Triad seen in Wilms tumor includes all except
a. Hematuria  
b. Fever  
c. Abdominal mass  
d. Hypertension  

**Answer:** d. Hypertension (not part of the classic triad)

**Explanation:** Classic triad of Wilms tumor: abdominal mass, fever, and hematuria. It is the most common renal neoplasm of childhood (commonly 2–5 years). Hypertension can occur due to renin secretion but is not classically listed in the triad.

### 3. A child has recurrent UTI without palpable urinary bladder. The best investigation would be
a. MCU (Micturating cystourethrogram)  
b. CT  
c. USG  
d. MRI  

**Answer:** a. MCU (Micturating cystourethrogram)

**Explanation:** Most common cause of recurrent UTI in children is vesicoureteral reflux (VUR). MCU detects VUR and other anomalies (e.g. posterior urethral valves). DMSA scan is useful for renal scarring/acute pyelonephritis. Definitive diagnosis of UTI depends on urine culture.

### 4. Commonest fracture in a fall with outstretched hand
a. Clavicle  
b. Pisiform  
c. Radius  
d. Scaphoid  

**Answer:** d. Scaphoid (commonly tested); distal radius (Colles) is also very common in adults.

**Explanation:** Fall on an outstretched hand (FOOSH) commonly causes scaphoid fracture (especially in young adults) and distal radius fracture. Clavicle is the most commonly fractured bone overall, often from a fall on the shoulder/outstretched hand, with fracture commonly at the junction of middle and outer third.

### 5. A 40-year-old female has a hard breast mass. Which simple investigation can help reach the diagnosis?
a. USG  
b. Mammography  
c. FNAC  
d. CT  

**Answer:** c. FNAC

**Explanation:** Triple assessment for breast cancer includes clinical examination, imaging (mammography/USG as appropriate), and tissue diagnosis (FNAC/core biopsy). FNAC is a simple investigation to help reach diagnosis. Mammography is the imaging of choice for breast screening (craniocaudal and mediolateral oblique views).

### 6. A patient has splenomegaly on palpation, hemoglobin 6 g%, and high total leukocyte count. Most probable diagnosis?
a. Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML)  
b. Hepatic anemia  
c. Aplastic anemia  
d. Multiple myeloma  

**Answer:** a. Chronic myeloid leukemia (CML)

**Explanation:** Marked leukocytosis with splenomegaly and anemia suggests leukemia (classically CML). Blood examination in leukemia may show anemia, variable WBC count (can be very high), thrombocytopenia, and blast cells in peripheral smear (diagnostic in acute leukemias).

### 7. Which of the following is a non-nucleated cell?
a. Lymphocyte  
b. RBC  
c. Neutrophil  
d. Monocyte  

**Answer:** b. RBC

**Explanation:** Mature RBCs are anucleate and lack organelles. They are biconcave with a large surface-area-to-volume ratio for gas exchange. Lifespan is about 120 days. Energy source is mainly glucose (glycolysis and HMP shunt). Reticulocytes are immature RBCs containing residual ribosomal RNA.

### 8. Which is the best mode of delivery in a woman with placenta previa and a dead fetus?
a. Cesarean section  
b. Prostaglandin induction / vaginal delivery  
c. Forceps delivery  
d. Expectant management  

**Answer:** b. Vaginal delivery (induction/expectant for vaginal delivery as appropriate)

**Explanation:** With placenta previa and a dead fetus, vaginal delivery is generally preferred if feasible to avoid cesarean morbidity, provided there is no severe hemorrhage or contraindication. Cesarean delivery is indicated for major placenta previa with significant bleeding, live fetus with indications, maternal instability, or obstruction. Active management is individualized based on bleeding, gestational age, and maternal condition.
