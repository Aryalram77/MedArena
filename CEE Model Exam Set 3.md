**1. Not included in femoral sheath is:**

*Answer: C — Femoral nerve*

The femoral sheath has three compartments: lateral (femoral artery), intermediate (femoral vein) and medial (femoral canal, containing lymph vessels and an occasional lymph node). The femoral nerve is not enclosed by the sheath; it lies lateral to the artery outside the sheath.

The salivary glands, liver, gallbladder and pancreas are not part of the digestive tract but take part in digestive activity, so they are accessory digestive organs. The spleen plays no role in digestion and is therefore not an accessory digestive organ.

**3. Rotator cuff doesn't include:**

*Answer: A — Teres major*

The rotator cuff is formed by supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor and subscapularis (SITS). Supraspinatus, infraspinatus and teres minor insert on the greater tubercle; subscapularis inserts on the lesser tubercle. Teres major is not part of the cuff.

*Answer: B — CN IX*

The glossopharyngeal nerve supplies both the ear region and the oral cavity. Pathology in the oral region can therefore present as referred pain to the ear.

**5. Fibers of spinal cord below L1 continue as:**

*Answer: A — Cauda equina*

The spinal cord ends caudally as the conus medullaris at the lower border of L1. Below this level the intradural nerve roots continue as the cauda equina in the subarachnoid space. The filum terminale is a non-neural fibrous extension reaching the coccyx.

**6. Percentage of blood supply to liver by portal vein is:**

*Answer: C — 75%*

The liver has a dual blood supply. The portal vein delivers approximately 75-80% of the blood flow, draining the spleen and gastrointestinal tract, while the hepatic artery supplies the remaining quarter. About half of the liver's oxygen demand is met by each source.

**7. Patient has painful hemorrhoids. Nerve carrying the pain sensation in hemorrhoids is:**

*Answer: A — Pudendal nerve*

Internal hemorrhoids lie above the pectinate line and are painless (autonomic supply, stretch sensation only). External hemorrhoids lie below the pectinate line, are covered by skin and are painful; the lower anal canal is supplied somatically by the pudendal nerve, which carries pain, temperature and touch.

**8. Which lemnisci carry pain, temperature and crude touch?**

*Answer: C — Spinal*

First order neurons ascend one or two segments and synapse in the dorsal horn. Second order neurons decussate in the anterior white commissure and run as the lateral spinothalamic tract in the cord, continuing in the brainstem as the spinal lemniscus. This pathway carries pain, temperature and crude touch.

Preganglionic axons arise in the superior salivatory nucleus in the pons, travel in the nervus intermedius and chorda tympani, join the lingual nerve and synapse in the submandibular ganglion. Postganglionic fibers supply the submandibular, sublingual and lingual glands.

*Answer: A — Cystic fibrosis*

Cystic fibrosis is an autosomal recessive defect of the CFTR gene on chromosome 7, commonly Phe508 deletion. CFTR reabsorbs chloride in sweat glands, so its failure raises sweat chloride. A pilocarpine iontophoresis sweat chloride test is diagnostic.

**13. In breastfeeding jaundice, increase in … is seen:**

*Answer: C — Unconjugated bilirubin*

Breastfeeding jaundice occurs in the first week as breastfeeding is established. Inadequate intake increases intestinal reabsorption of bilirubin and delays passage of meconium, raising unconjugated bilirubin. More frequent feeding reduces the risk.

Vitamin K dependent coagulation factors are synthesized in the liver, and cholestasis also impairs absorption of vitamin K. Factor V does not depend on vitamin K while factor VII does, so measuring both helps separate parenchymal dysfunction from vitamin K malabsorption.

*Answer: A — Vitamin K*

In obstructive jaundice prothrombin falls both from impaired hepatic synthesis and from reduced intestinal absorption of vitamin K. Vitamin K supplementation corrects the deficiency and improves coagulation.

In hemolytic jaundice the load of unconjugated bilirubin is handled by the liver, so no bilirubin appears in urine, while increased urobilinogen is excreted. In hepatic and obstructive jaundice bilirubin is present in urine.

*Answer: B — Autoclave*

Autoclaving with saturated steam under pressure, typically 121 degrees Celsius at 15 lb per square inch for 15-20 minutes, is the surest method of destroying bacterial spores. Boiling and pasteurization do not reliably kill spores, and disinfection targets vegetative forms only.

**45. Cough while using ACE inhibitors is due to?**

*Answer: C — Decreased breakdown of bradykinin*

Angiotensin converting enzyme degrades bradykinin. Inhibiting ACE reduces bradykinin breakdown, raising plasma bradykinin, which activates pro-inflammatory peptides such as substance P and triggers local histamine release and dry cough.

**46. Iodine is given before thyroid surgery in order to:**

*Answer: D — Decrease vascularity of the gland*

Iodine is the fastest acting thyroid inhibitor and inhibits hormone release, but the gland escapes within days. Given for about ten days before thyroidectomy in Graves disease, it makes the gland firm, less vascular and easier to operate on. Propranolol is used for rapid symptom control.

Dihydropyridines such as nifedipine and amlodipine are potent vasodilators that cause marked reflex sympathetic stimulation with tachycardia. **164. Which of the following is the surest sign of compartment syndrome**

*Answer: A — Pain out of proportion*

The surest sign of compartment syndrome is pain out of proportion to the injury, with pain on passive stretch of the muscles in the compartment and a tense compartment. Pulselessness, pallor, paraesthesia and paralysis are late features. Intracompartmental pressure above 40 mm Hg confirms it.

<sub>Source page 48</sub>

**165. Surgery is done for intertrochanteric fracture because;**

*Answer: D — To promote early mobilization*

Intertrochanteric fractures unite readily, unlike femoral neck fractures, and the aim is to maintain a normal neck-shaft angle during union. In elderly patients internal fixation is preferred because prolonged traction of three to four months causes complications of recumbency such as bed sores and pneumonia, so early mobilization is the reason for surgery.

<sub>Source page 49</sub>

**166. Mallampati grading is used for:**

*Answer: A — Assessment of Airway*

The Mallampati classification predicts the ease of intubation. Class I shows soft palate, uvula, fauces and pillars; class II shows soft palate, uvula and fauces; class III shows soft palate and base of uvula; class IV shows only hard palate. A high score predicts difficult intubation and a higher incidence of sleep apnoea.

<sub>Source page 49</sub>

**167. Extent of spread of spinal anesthesia is determined by all except:**

*Answer: D — Presence of epinephrine*

The three most important modifiable factors determining the spread of spinal anaesthesia are the baricity of the local anaesthetic, the position of the patient during and just after injection, and the dose injected. The presence of epinephrine prolongs the block but does not determine its extent.

<sub>Source page 49</sub>

**168. Best management for difficult intubation and difficult bagging is:**

*Answer: A — Scalpel Cricothyrotomy*

When a patient cannot be intubated and cannot be ventilated, the ASA 2022 guideline calls for an emergency invasive airway. Surgical cricothyrotomy is the best option; needle cricothyrotomy with a pressure regulated device, large bore cannula cricothyrotomy and surgical tracheostomy are alternatives.

<sub>Source page 49</sub>

**169. 10 pint blood transfusion is done in a patient. What is the likely electrolyte disbalance that is seen?**

*Answer: C — Hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia*

Massive transfusion causes hyperkalaemia, hypocalcaemia and hypomagnesaemia, with hyperammonaemia, hypothermia and acidosis. Dilutional coagulopathy and DIC are the most worrisome problems and the usual cause of death after massive transfusion.

<sub>Source page 50</sub>

**170. Patient was undergoing cardiac arrest. You notice that he has tension pneumothorax. What to do?**

*Answer: B — Needle thoracotomy*

In cardiac arrest with tension pneumothorax, immediate needle decompression is required because chest compressions cannot be effective while intrathoracic pressure is raised. Chest X-ray would only delay treatment.

<sub>Source page 51</sub>

**171. Patient is having OCD with predominant obsessions. He doesn't want pharmacotherapy. What is the preferred non pharmacological treatment in this case?**

*Answer: A — Exposure and response prevention*

Exposure and response prevention is the gold standard psychotherapy for OCD. It exposes the patient to obsessional triggers while preventing the compulsive response, so anxiety is gradually tolerated. Systematic desensitization is used for phobias, thought blocking is not recommended, and mindfulness is not first line.

<sub>Source page 51</sub>

**172. A 12 year old girl has multiple fainting episodes lasting for upto 30 minutes with no bowel or bladder incontinence. Diagnosis is:**

*Answer: A — Conversion disorder*

Conversion disorder converts psychological distress into physical symptoms. Features pointing to it here are repeated episodes, a duration of up to 30 minutes which is far longer than organic syncope, and absence of bowel or bladder incontinence.

<sub>Source page 51</sub>

**173. A patient after RTA is unable to recall past events. This is**

*Answer: B — Retrograde amnesia*

Retrograde amnesia is loss of memory for events before the incident. Anterograde amnesia is inability to form new memories after it, and confabulation is fabrication of memories to fill gaps.

<sub>Source page 51</sub>

**174. A patient who previously used to consume alcohol becomes disoriented in ICU. What is diagnosis?**

*Answer: A — Delirium*

Delirium is acute confusion and disorientation developing rapidly, commonly triggered by medical illness, medication, infection or substance withdrawal. Alcohol withdrawal may contribute, but the acute disorientation itself is delirium.

<sub>Source page 52</sub>

**175. A patient took Haloperidol from pharmacy due to anxiety and tension. Now she is unable to retract tongue back. What is the likely diagnosis?**

*Answer: A — Extrapyramidal symptoms*

Haloperidol, a typical antipsychotic, blocks dopamine and can cause extrapyramidal symptoms. Acute dystonia is a hallmark, presenting as involuntary muscle contraction such as the tongue protrusion described here. Akathisia is restlessness, and neuroleptic malignant syndrome causes fever, rigidity and autonomic instability.

<sub>Source page 52</sub>

**176. The main reason for avoiding the combined administration of levodopa and an inhibitor of both forms of monoamine oxidase is:**

*Answer: B — Hypertensive emergency*

Monoamine oxidase inhibitors prevent breakdown of dopamine and other neurotransmitters. Combined with levodopa, which is converted to dopamine, this causes excessive neurotransmitter levels and a sharp rise in blood pressure, producing a hypertensive crisis.

<sub>Source page 52</sub>

**177. A patient was scolded in office by his boss. Goes home and is nasty to his wife. What is the defense mechanism?**

*Answer: A — Displacement*

Displacement redirects emotion from its original source onto a less threatening target, here from the boss to the wife. Sublimation channels impulses into acceptable activity, rationalization justifies behaviour, and projection attributes one's own feelings to others.

<sub>Source page 53</sub>

**178. Which one of the following will have the highest risk of suicide?**

*Answer: A — Mood disorders*

Mood disorders, particularly major depressive disorder and bipolar disorder, carry the highest suicide risk among psychiatric diagnoses. Contributing factors are symptom severity, hopelessness, impulsivity and psychosocial stressors. Alcohol use disorder is a significant risk factor but lower than mood disorders.

<sub>Source page 53</sub>

**179. Bat wing appearance of pulmonary edema is a feature of:**

*Answer: A — Congestive heart failure*

The bat wing appearance is the characteristic pattern of pulmonary oedema on chest X-ray in congestive heart failure. Failure to pump efficiently causes backup of fluid in the lungs, giving diffuse bilateral opacities more prominent centrally and in the upper zones.

<sub>Source page 53</sub>

**180. X-ray showed gas in both inside and outside wall of intestine. Likely diagnosis is:**

*Answer: B — Pneumoperitoneum*

Gas inside and outside the bowel wall indicates that air has escaped from the gastrointestinal tract into the peritoneal cavity, which is pneumoperitoneum. Causes include perforated ulcer, trauma, bowel ischaemia and iatrogenic injury.

<sub>Source page 54</sub>

**181. The best way for prevention of contrast nephropathy is:**

*Answer: A — Hydration*

Adequate hydration before and after contrast maintains renal perfusion and promotes excretion of contrast, and remains the universally recommended strategy. N-acetylcysteine evidence is weak, and dopamine and adrenaline have no consistent benefit.

<sub>Source page 54</sub>

**182. Investigation of choice for patient with high likelihood of pulmonary embolism is:**

*Answer: B — CT angiography*

CT pulmonary angiography is the investigation of choice when clinical likelihood of pulmonary embolism is high, being highly sensitive and specific for filling defects in the pulmonary arteries. D-dimer is sensitive but non-specific, and V/Q scanning has largely been replaced.

<sub>Source page 54</sub>

**183. ERCP is used for?**

*Answer: A — CBD stone*

Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography is used to visualize and remove stones lodged in the common bile duct, relieving obstruction and preventing pancreatitis. Gallstones are managed by cholecystectomy, and ERCP plays no role in colon cancer or gastric ulcer.

<sub>Source page 55</sub>

**184. Hounsfield unit of 0 is seen in:**

*Answer: C — Water*

Water is the baseline reference on the Hounsfield scale at 0 HU. Fat ranges about -50 to -100, air about -1000, and bone from about +700 to +3000 HU.

<sub>Source page 55</sub>

**185. Which investigation helps to visualize terminal ileum?**

*Answer: D — Ba enema*

A barium enema introduces contrast through the rectum to outline the colon, and reflux into the terminal ileum allows it to be visualized where it joins the caecum. A barium meal examines the oesophagus, stomach and duodenum, and a follow through assesses the small bowel but visualizes the terminal ileum less clearly.

<sub>Source page 55</sub>

**186. Opacity of right border of heart denotes consolidation at:**

*Answer: C — Superior segment of the right lower lobe*

The right heart border is formed by the right atrium and is in contact with the medial segment of the right middle lobe. Loss of the right heart border, the silhouette sign, therefore indicates consolidation in the medial segment of the right middle lobe. The superior segment of the right lower lobe lies posteriorly and does not border the heart.

<sub>Source page 56</sub>

**187. Minimum duration of fasting before hepatobiliary USG is:**

*Answer: B — 6 hours*

Fasting for at least 6 hours reduces gas and bowel activity, improving visualization of the liver, gallbladder and bile ducts and the diagnostic accuracy of the examination.

<sub>Source page 56</sub>

**188. Soap bubble appearance is seen in:**

*Answer: A — GCT*

Soap bubble appearance is characteristic of giant cell tumour, which occurs in the distal femur. Osteosarcoma shows sunray appearance, Codman triangle and hair on end appearance, Ewing sarcoma shows onion peel appearance, and osteoid osteoma shows a lytic lesion with a zone of sclerosis.

<sub>Source page 56</sub>

**189. Which of the following will prevent misuse of drug?**

*Answer: A — Standard treatment protocol*

A standard treatment protocol sets out guidelines that practitioners follow for specific conditions, ensuring consistent evidence based care and preventing inappropriate or misused prescribing. A formulary and an essential drug list manage availability and cost rather than preventing misuse.

<sub>Source page 56</sub>

**190. Establishment of Drug Information Resource Center will lead to:**

*Answer: A — Education about drug and drug usage*

A drug information resource centre provides accurate, validated, unbiased and up to date information on medicines to health professionals and the public. It does not prescribe, substitute a prescribed medicine, or advertise any brand.

<sub>Source page 57</sub>

**191. Proper feedback should be:**

*Answer: A — Specific and timely*

Effective feedback is related to agreed goals, specific, reinforces positive behaviour, gives guidelines for improvement, is limited to remediable behaviour, is first hand and objective, timely, descriptive rather than evaluative, and includes an action plan.

<sub>Source page 57</sub>

**192. Doctor should be truthful to patient due to which ethical principle:**

*Answer: A — Autonomy*

Autonomy allows an individual freedom to make their own decisions, and truthfulness respects that freedom. Beneficence means doing good, non-maleficence means doing no harm, and informed consent is a process rather than a principle.

<sub>Source page 57</sub>

**193. Which one of the following is not useful in a child with no pulse and ECG shows normal P and QRS waves?**

*Answer: A — DC Synchronized shock*

With normal P and QRS complexes the rhythm is not shockable, so there is no role for synchronized DC shock. Pulseless electrical activity is managed with CPR, adrenaline and correction of reversible causes.

<sub>Source page 57</sub>

**194. A patient was unconscious. Emergency OT had to be done. No patient party. OT was done. Reasoning behind it?**

*Answer: B — Implied consent*

The doctrine of implied consent holds that harm caused in good faith without consent is not an offence where the person cannot signify consent and there is no guardian from whom consent can be obtained in time. In an emergency the law implies consent. Therapeutic privilege allows withholding information, and therapeutic waiver is a competent patient giving up the right to be informed.

<sub>Source page 59</sub>

**195. True regarding research in prisoners is:**

*Answer: A — Prisoners have similar rights as normal people in case of research*

For prisoners, armed forces, police, students and employees, the research protocol must include additional safeguards so that participation is free of coercion and without negative repercussions for refusal or withdrawal. Their rights are not removed, and a warden cannot consent on their behalf.

<sub>Source page 59</sub>

**196. Why is rescue breath given before compression in children?**

*Answer: B — Cause of cardiac arrest is hypoxia*

Hypoxia is a common cause of cardiac arrest in children, so rescue breaths are given first to correct it. Compressions follow at 100-120 per minute, depressing the chest at least a third of its depth, in cycles of 30 compressions to 2 breaths.

<sub>Source page 59</sub>

**197. A manager wants low resource wasting. What principle does he follow?**

*Answer: A — Efficiency*

Efficiency measures the relationship between results achieved and the effort expended in money, resources and time, that is output over input. Efficacy is usefulness under ideal controlled conditions, and effectiveness is usefulness in real community situations.

<sub>Source page 59</sub>

**198. Child was having peanut butter cookies. Then he developed diarrhea, wheezing. What will be the drug?**

*Answer: A — IM Adrenaline*

This is anaphylaxis, and the treatment of first choice is intramuscular adrenaline 0.3-0.5 mL of 1:1000, repeated every 5-20 minutes as needed. Cutaneous features are the commonest presentation, and laryngeal oedema or bronchospasm may follow.

<sub>Source page 59</sub>

**199. Death due measles, IV drug users, RTA cases. What will denote all these?**

*Answer: C — Mortality rate*

Mortality rate is the number of deaths in a given population during a specific period, expressed per 1,000 or 100,000 people, and it applies to measles deaths, deaths among intravenous drug users and road traffic fatalities alike. Case fatality rate is the proportion of diagnosed cases who die, and morbidity rate measures disease occurrence.

<sub>Source page 60</sub>

**200. Which of the following is basic skill?**

*Answer: B — Communication skill*

Communication skill is a basic skill for all medical practitioners. It improves personal performance, strengthens relationships with patients and supports career progression, unlike procedural skills such as cannulation, lumbar puncture or surgery.

<sub>Source page 60</sub>
